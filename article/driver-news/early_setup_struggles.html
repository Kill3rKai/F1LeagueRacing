Paddock Talk: Who's Struggling With Setup Early On?

2 days ago · Season 1

Three races into Season 1 and the paddock already has a recurring talking point: Ashigaru’s Red Bull and its setup. The results on paper don’t tell the full story — but the pattern across Australia, China and Japan is hard to ignore.

Australia — no fair test

The season opener never gave a clean read on the car. A multi-car collision ahead left debris on track, puncturing rear tyres for several drivers including Kill3rKai. Ashigaru himself suffered a rear puncture and ended up beached in a gravel trap. With the race ended early for him, there was nothing useful to take away on balance or aero — just another DNF on the board.

China — pace without the tools

Qualifying told its own story: P15. From there Ashigaru produced a strong recovery drive, climbing all the way to third. The problem was what waited ahead. Kill3rKai’s Ferrari and Polaris’ McLaren were running their own recovery strategy together, and the Red Bull found itself stuck behind that pair for long stretches. The underlying setup still wasn’t right — the climb showed the driver could extract results, but the car wasn’t giving him a platform to attack once he reached the cars in front.

Japan — dirty air exposes everything

Japan was the clearest evidence yet that the setup is off. Ashigaru’s car simply could not handle dirty air from the cars ahead. Every time he closed up for an overtake, the loss of front aero left the rear relatively high — a classic imbalance that made the car unstable the moment he needed it most. Instead of completing the move, he was shunted before he could even attempt one.

That left him trapped behind a Ferrari for the majority of the race. The only way past came when that Ferrari took too much kerb and got shunted itself. It was a podium-contending recovery ruined by a car that refused to cooperate in traffic.

Three races, same question

Australia was impossible to judge. China showed recovery pace with no clean air to use it. Japan confirmed the aero balance is wrong the moment another car is within a second. Across all three weekends the verdict from the garage and the paddock is the same: the setups haven’t been good enough.

With Miami next, the pressure is on Red Bull to find a direction that lets Ashigaru race the cars in front instead of fighting his own rear end every time he gets close. The driver has shown he can climb the order. The car still has to let him finish the job.
