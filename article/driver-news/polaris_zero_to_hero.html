Polaris, From Zero To Hero?

2 days ago · Season 1

Over the last season, Polaris’ best performance came at Baku. That win, however, arrived only after every other human driver had retired. It was a result that raised more questions than it answered — and those questions followed him straight into Season 1.

A nightmare start in Australia

Round 1 in Australia could hardly have gone worse. Critical diffuser damage left Polaris with no choice but to retire, handing him a DNF and a poor opening statement for the new campaign. For a driver still trying to prove last year’s Baku result wasn’t a fluke, it was the worst possible start.

China: the fightback begins

Heading into Round 2, something looked different. Starting from the back of the grid, Polaris carved his way up to P4 before contact with Kill3rKai dropped him down the order and earned him a 10-second penalty. Most drivers would have folded. Instead he clawed back from 21st, working alongside Kill3rKai for stretches of the race, and finished a comfortable fifth. The recovery drive turned heads.

Japan: the breakthrough

Japan is where the season truly started to take shape. A strong qualifying performance put him sixth on the grid. A red-flag incident and a trip through the gravel dumped him as low as 13th, yet by the chequered flag he had climbed all the way back to fourth — still chasing the leaders into the final corner. Three races, three very different stories, and a clear upward trajectory.

Hardware headaches

The Japanese result came despite technical issues that could have ended anyone else’s afternoon. After the race Polaris was candid about the problems he faced.

“My wheel was sliding off my stand. I had to nurse a broken wheel clamp till the end.” — Polaris

There is speculation that a new wheel is on the way, though no timeline has been confirmed. In a league where margins are already tight, reliable hardware could be the difference between another recovery drive and a genuine shot at the podium.

The noise from the paddock

Not everyone is convinced. Rival drivers have been less than complimentary, with some openly describing him as “shit” and claiming he “belongs in F2.” Whether that is genuine assessment or classic paddock needle remains to be seen — but the results on track are starting to make the criticism harder to justify.

Miami awaits

Three rounds in, Polaris has gone from a first-lap retirement to a driver who can start near the back, survive chaos, and still finish in the points — or higher. The question now is simple: can he carry this form into Miami, or will the pressure and the lingering hardware doubts catch up with him?

The next race will go a long way toward answering it.
