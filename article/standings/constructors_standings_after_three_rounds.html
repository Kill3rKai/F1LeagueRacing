Constructors' Standings After Three Rounds

6 days ago · Season 1

The drivers' championship has grabbed most of the headlines through the opening rounds, but quietly, in the background, Red Bull has been building something just as interesting. Three rounds in, they sit on top of the constructors' table — and the way they got there says a lot about how this season is shaping up.

Australia — a foothold, not a statement

Round 1 was never going to be a clean read for anyone. With Ashigaru caught up in the opening-lap debris and beached in the gravel, Red Bull's afternoon looked over almost before it started. Instead, the sister car quietly picked up the pieces, banking 4 points in a race that could easily have returned nothing at all.

China — the sprint, then the statement

China is where the tally really started to move. The sprint added another 9 points to the total, nudging Red Bull up to 13 on the season. Then came the Grand Prix itself, and it was the team's best result by far — 18 and 15 points landed from the two cars, a combined 33-point haul in a single race that pushed the running total to 46.

Japan — consistency over chaos

Japan was the wildest race of the season so far, and Red Bull didn't come away from it unscathed either — but the numbers held up. 6 points from one car and 18 from the other took the team to 70 points after three rounds, the platform they now carry into Miami.

Points By Round

  1. Round 1 — Australia: +4 (running total: 4)
  2. Round 2 — China Sprint: +9 (running total: 13)
  3. Round 2 — China Grand Prix: +18 & +15 (running total: 46)
  4. Round 3 — Japan: +6 & +18 (running total: 70)

The bigger picture

None of Red Bull's three weekends so far have been drama-free — a first-lap DNF, a car that's been fighting its own setup in traffic, and a chaotic Japan that could have gone very differently. What's kept the total climbing isn't a single standout result, it's that both cars have kept finding points almost every time out.

With the rest of the grid still chasing bigger individual results of their own, 70 points after three rounds is a total the other constructors will need to start taking seriously heading into Miami.
